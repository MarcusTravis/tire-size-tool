275/65R18 vs 275/70R18 tire size comparison

275/70R18 is 1.08 in (27.5 mm) taller than 275/65R18 (+3.38%). Ground clearance at the axle changes by about +0.54 in. At an indicated 60 mph you would really be going 62.0 mph. That is more than a 3% change in diameter, so check clearance, gearing and speedometer calibration.

What changes

Both are the same width, so the difference is all in the sidewall. Both mount on a 18 in wheel.

Each mile, 275/65R18 turns about 629 times and 275/70R18 about 608 times. Fewer turns per mile means the speedometer and odometer read low by roughly 3.4% unless they are recalibrated, and the effective final drive ratio gets taller, which can feel like slightly less acceleration.

These figures are calculated from the size alone using the standard tire size formulas. Actual tires vary by brand and model, and fitment depends on your wheels and suspension, so check with a tire shop before buying.

Quick answers

Is 275/70R18 bigger than 275/65R18?

Yes. 275/70R18 is 33.16 in tall and 275/65R18 is 32.07 in, so 275/70R18 is 1.08 in taller (+3.4%). Both are the same width, so the difference is all in the sidewall.

How far off will my speedometer be with 275/70R18 instead of 275/65R18?

About 3.4%. If the speedometer is set for 275/65R18, it reads low: an indicated 60 mph is really 62.0 mph, and 70 mph is really 72.4 mph. The odometer is off by the same percentage.

Is 275/65R18 to 275/70R18 within the 3% guideline?

No. The 3.4% change in diameter is more than the common rule of thumb of about 3%. That does not mean it will not fit, but check clearance, gearing and speedometer recalibration, and confirm fitment with a tire shop.
